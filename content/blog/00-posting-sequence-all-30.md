# 998 Web Designs — 30-Article Posting Sequence

**Cadence: 2 posts per week (Tuesday + Thursday). Full run = 15 weeks (~3.5 months).**
After Week 15, drop to 1 post every 1–2 weeks, indefinitely, to keep the freshness signal alive.

## Why this order
Sequenced by **buyer intent**, not topic. Early weeks front-load posts that catch a homeowner's-business-owner who is close to hiring or actively frustrated ("not showing up on Google," "how to get leads," "what does it cost"). Middle weeks build topical authority across local SEO, conversion, and trust. Later weeks cover broader, educational, and seasonal/top-of-funnel pieces that benefit from already having indexed, linked pages around them.

Each post should: (1) be submitted in Google Search Console the day it goes live, (2) internally link to 2–3 other posts and to your service/pricing pages, and (3) link out to the relevant add-on page where natural (AI receptionist, chatbot, booking, reviews).

## Internal linking map (do this as you publish)

Link each new post back to already-published ones so authority compounds:

- All GBP / local-SEO posts → link to each other (#1, #5, #9, #21, #26, #30-cluster)
- All cost / ROI / vendor posts → link to each other (#3, #15, #19, #20, #28)
- All conversion / design posts → link to each other (#6, #7, #11, #17, #25)
- Every post → link to your **/pricing** and **/start** pages at least once
- Add-on posts (#16, #23, #24) → link to the matching add-on section on the homepage

## After the 30 run out
The single most important thing: **do not stop.** One post every 1–2 weeks indefinitely keeps the site looking alive and keeps compounding. Topic ideas for the next backlog: seasonal maintenance questions homeowners ask (which become "[service] FAQ" pages), individual town deep-dives, and "[specific job] cost in [town]" pages that double as both blog and ranking content.
