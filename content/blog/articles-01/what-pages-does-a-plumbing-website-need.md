# What pages does a plumbing website actually need?

*Not thirty. The right five to eight pages do more for a plumber than a sprawling site nobody maintains. Here is the structure that books jobs.*

The instinct is to add pages. More services, more topics, more is better. For a local plumber, the opposite is true. A tight site that loads fast and answers the homeowner's question beats a thirty-page site that goes stale by the second month.

Here is the core structure, in priority order.

## The pages every plumbing site needs

1. **Home.** Who you are, where you work, the phone number above the fold, and one clear next step. A homeowner should know within five seconds that you handle their problem in their area.
2. **Services.** Either one strong page or a short hub linking to your main jobs - drain cleaning, water heaters, leak repair, repiping, emergency service. Each describes the problem in the homeowner's words, not trade jargon.
3. **Service area / town pages.** The pages that win "[service] in [town]" searches. One per town you genuinely want to drive to. This is where local ranking is won or lost.
4. **About.** License number, years in business, a real photo of you or the crew. This is the trust page that closes the referral.
5. **Reviews / testimonials.** Recent, specific, ideally pulled live from Google. Proof beats promises.
6. **Contact.** Phone, a short form, service hours, and emergency availability. Tap-to-call on mobile.

## The pages worth adding once the core works

- **Emergency plumbing** - a fast-loading page for the burst-pipe-at-night searcher who decides in seconds.
- **Financing** - if you offer it, the big jobs (repipe, sewer line) close better with a payment path visible.
- **A small set of job galleries** - before-and-after photos of real work.

## Pages most plumbers do not need

- **A blog you will not maintain.** An abandoned blog looks worse than no blog. Only commit if you will keep it fed (or have someone do it).
- **A page for every micro-service.** One faucet-repair page is fine. Twelve near-identical pages dilute each other.
- **A long company-history essay.** Homeowners want to know you are licensed and reachable, not your founding story.

## Why structure matters more than page count

Every page should map to a thing a homeowner searches and a job you want. Service pages catch "what's wrong." Town pages catch "near me." The About and Reviews pages convert the visitor a referral or the map sent your way. That is the whole machine - five to eight pages, each with a job.
