# What is a good website actually worth to a plumber? The math.

*A site is an expense until you run the numbers. Here is a simple way to estimate what added calls are worth to your specific business.*

A website feels like a cost because you pay for it once, up front, and the return shows up later in calls you cannot easily trace. That is why so many contractors treat it as an expense to minimize rather than an investment to size correctly. A little math fixes that.

## Start with your numbers

You need three figures you already know:

1. **Your average job value.** What does a typical job bring in?
2. **Your close rate.** Of the people who call, how many book?
3. **Your rough margin.** What is left after costs?

Now the only unknown is how many extra calls a good site brings - and even a conservative guess makes the case.

## A conservative example

Say your average job is $400 and you close half the calls. Two extra qualified calls a week is one booked job - about $400 a week, or roughly $20,000 a year in new revenue from two calls a week you were not getting before.

A flat $7,998 site pays for itself in the first few months at that rate, and keeps producing after. For a roofer, where a single job can run several thousand dollars, the math is even more lopsided - one extra job can cover the entire build.

## Why the calls were not happening

The homeowner searching "plumber near me" or "roof leak repair" at 9pm is ready to hire. If you do not appear, or you appear with a slow or thin site, that call goes to a competitor. You were not losing those calls because of price or quality - you were losing them because you were not findable or not credible at the moment of the search.

## The honest caveat

A site is not a slot machine. It will not produce if it is buried, slow, or never updated - and it works alongside your Google profile and reviews, not instead of them. But built right and pointed at the jobs you want, the question stops being "can I afford a website" and becomes "can I afford to keep missing the calls it would catch."

Run your own numbers. For most plumbers and roofers, the break-even is a handful of jobs, and everything after is profit.
