# Do I need a website, or is a Facebook page enough for my trade business?

*A Facebook page is free and easy. It is also rented land you do not control. Here is the honest tradeoff for a plumber or roofer deciding between the two.*

Plenty of contractors run their whole web presence off a Facebook page. It is free, it is familiar, and customers are already there. So the question is fair: do you actually need a website too?

The short answer: a Facebook page is fine as a supplement and risky as your only home base. Here is why.

## What a Facebook page does well

- It is free and quick to set up.
- It is good for posting recent jobs and staying visible to past customers.
- Reviews and recommendations there carry social weight.
- People who already know your name can find you easily.

If that were the whole picture, you could stop reading. It is not.

## What a Facebook page cannot do

1. **Rank for "near me" searches.** When a homeowner searches "roofer near me" on Google, your Facebook page almost never shows up the way a real website with service and town pages does.
2. **Be controlled by you.** Facebook owns the platform, the rules, and the reach. They can change the algorithm, restrict your page, or bury your posts - and you have no recourse.
3. **Convert a serious buyer.** A homeowner spending thousands on a roof wants to see a credible, professional site with proof, not scroll a social feed for your phone number.
4. **Be an asset you can sell.** A website with its own domain and lead flow is worth money if you ever sell the business. A Facebook page is not really yours to sell.

## The rented-land problem

This is the core issue. Building your business entirely on Facebook is building on rented land. It works until the landlord changes the terms - and they have, repeatedly. A website is the land you own. Everything else points to it.

## The right setup

Use both, in the right roles. Keep the Facebook page for staying visible to people who know you and posting recent work. Make the website your home base - the thing that ranks, converts the serious buyer, and that you actually control. The Facebook page feeds it; the website closes the job. We build the part you own.
