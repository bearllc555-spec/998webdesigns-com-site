# How long does it take to build a contractor website?

*Some agencies take three months. Some builds take a week. Here is what actually drives the timeline - and what makes a project drag.*

"A few weeks" is the answer most contractors get, and it is the answer that turns into a few months. The honest version: the build itself is fast. What stretches a website project is rarely the design work - it is the back-and-forth.

## What actually takes time

1. **Getting your content.** The single biggest delay. Photos of real jobs, your service list, your license number, the towns you serve. A builder cannot finish what you have not handed over.
2. **Decisions.** Every "let me think about it" adds days. Projects move at the speed of the slowest approval.
3. **Scope creep.** "Can we also add..." resets the clock. A fixed scope is what keeps a timeline honest.
4. **Revisions.** One or two focused rounds are normal. Endless tweaking is what turns weeks into months.
5. **Domain and hosting setup.** Usually quick, occasionally slow if a domain is locked at an old provider you cannot access.

## A realistic timeline by build type

- **DIY:** as long as your evenings allow - often a month or more of stop-and-start, frequently never quite finished.
- **Cheap freelancer:** two to six weeks, highly variable, depends on how booked they are.
- **Flat-fee specialist:** about a week to two, because the scope is fixed and the process is repeatable.
- **Full agency:** six to twelve weeks, sometimes more, because the process is built for bigger, custom projects.

## How to make any build go faster

1. **Gather content before you start.** Twenty good job photos, your services, your towns, your license and insurance details. Have it ready and you cut the timeline in half.
2. **Make sure you control your domain.** Know your registrar login. A locked domain is the most common avoidable delay.
3. **Decide who approves.** One decision-maker moves faster than a committee of three.
4. **Keep revisions in batches.** One list of changes beats ten one-line emails.
