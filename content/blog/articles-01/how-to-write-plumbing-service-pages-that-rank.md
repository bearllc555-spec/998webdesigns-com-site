# How to write plumbing service pages that actually rank

*A page titled "Services" with a paragraph of jargon ranks for nothing. Here is how to structure service pages a homeowner and Google both understand.*

Most plumbing sites have one page called "Services" with a wall of text and a list of bullet points. It reads like an internal memo and it ranks for nothing. The fix is not more words - it is the right structure, repeated for each job you want.

## Why one combined page fails

When drain cleaning, water heater installation, and repiping all live on one page, Google cannot tell what the page is about, so it ranks it for nothing in particular. A homeowner searching "water heater replacement near me" needs a page about water heater replacement - not a paragraph buried under four other services.

One page per major service. That is the whole idea.

## What each service page needs

1. **A title that matches the search.** "Water Heater Repair & Replacement in [Town]" beats "Our Services." The words a homeowner types should appear in the heading.
2. **The problem in plain language.** Start with the homeowner's situation - "No hot water this morning?" - not your company history.
3. **What you actually do.** The specific work, in terms a non-plumber understands. Skip the trade jargon.
4. **Why you, briefly.** Licensed, insured, same-day where possible, upfront pricing. Two or three trust points.
5. **Proof.** A real photo of that kind of job, a review that mentions it.
6. **One clear next step.** Phone number and a short form. Tap-to-call on mobile.

## The mistakes that kill rankings

- **Thin pages.** Two sentences will not rank. Aim for genuinely useful depth, not padding.
- **Identical pages.** Copying one service page and swapping a word makes near-duplicates that compete with each other. Each should be genuinely different.
- **No mention of where you work.** If the page never names your towns, it will not rank for local searches.
- **Burying the phone number.** The page can rank perfectly and still fail if the homeowner cannot find how to call.

## How this connects to the rest of the site

Service pages catch "what's wrong" searches. Town pages catch "near me." They link to each other - a service page mentions the towns it covers, a town page links to the services offered there. That internal linking is part of why a structured site outranks a one-pager.
