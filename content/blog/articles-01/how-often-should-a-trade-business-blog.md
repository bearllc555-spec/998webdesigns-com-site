# How often should a plumbing or roofing business blog, if at all?

*Most contractors either never blog or start and quit. Here is whether a blog is worth it for a trade business and how to do it without it eating your time.*

The honest truth about blogging for a trade business: most contractors should not start one unless they will actually keep it going. An abandoned blog - three posts from two years ago - looks worse than no blog at all. But done consistently, it is one of the better long-term local SEO plays. The question is whether you can sustain it.

## Why a blog can help a contractor

- Each post is another page that can rank for a specific homeowner question ("why is my water pressure low," "how long does a roof last").
- It signals to Google that your site is active, which helps everything rank.
- It answers the questions homeowners search before they hire, putting you in front of them early.
- It builds topical authority over months, which compounds.

## Why most contractor blogs fail

They start with enthusiasm, publish four posts, and stop when the busy season hits. A stale blog signals an abandoned business. The failure is almost never the idea - it is the lack of a sustainable system.

## The realistic cadence

For a trade business, **one to two posts a month, consistently, forever** beats a burst of ten and then silence. Consistency over months and years is what moves rankings - not volume in a single week. If you can only sustain one a month, do one a month and never miss.

## How to do it without it eating your time

1. **Answer real questions.** The ones homeowners actually ask you on jobs. You already know them.
2. **Keep them short and useful.** A genuinely helpful 500-700 words beats a padded 2,000.
3. **Batch or delegate.** Either write several at once in a quiet stretch, or hand it to someone whose job is to keep it fed. The owner writing posts at 11pm is how blogs die.
4. **Link posts to your service and town pages.** That internal linking is part of what makes the blog help your rankings, not just sit there.

## The honest recommendation

If you will keep it fed, blog. If you know yourself and you will not, do not start - put that energy into your Google profile and reviews instead. The worst outcome is the half-built blog that signals neglect.
