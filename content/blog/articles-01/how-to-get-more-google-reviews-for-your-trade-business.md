# How to get more Google reviews for your plumbing or roofing business

*Reviews are the strongest free signal in local search, and most contractors leave them on the table. Here is a system that actually gets them.*

Two things are true about reviews. They are one of the biggest factors in whether you show up in the map pack and whether a homeowner picks you over the contractor next to you. And almost every plumber and roofer we meet is leaving them to chance.

"Chance" means you get a review when a customer is unusually thrilled or unusually angry. The steady stream that actually moves the needle requires a system - but the system is simple.

## Why reviews matter more than the star count

It is not about a perfect five. A homeowner trusts twenty recent, specific reviews far more than two hundred old ones or a flawless score that looks too clean. Google feels the same way. **Recency and specificity** beat volume and perfection. "Replaced our water heater same day, clean and on time" does more than "great service."

## The system that actually works

1. **Ask at the moment of relief.** The best time is the second the job is done and it worked - the leak stopped, the roof is whole. Wait until the next day and the feeling fades. Ask while it is fresh.
2. **Make it one tap.** Do not say "look us up on Google." Text a direct review link. Every extra step loses people. The easier you make it, the more you get.
3. **Have the tech ask, with the owner's link.** The person who did the work asking face-to-face, then a text with the link before they pull away, converts far better than an email a week later.
4. **Give a reason and a nudge.** "It really helps a small local business like ours" works. People help when they understand it matters.
5. **Respond to every review.** A short thank-you on the good ones, a calm professional reply on the bad ones. Future customers read how you handle the worst review more closely than the best.

## What not to do

- **Never buy reviews or post fake ones.** Google catches it, and the penalty is worse than having few reviews.
- **Do not offer a discount for a review.** That violates Google's rules and can get your reviews stripped.
- **Do not batch-blast old customers.** A pile of reviews appearing in one day looks engineered. A steady weekly trickle looks real - because it is.

## How your website helps

Pull your best Google reviews onto the site so they work twice - once in search, again when a homeowner is deciding. A review widget that updates automatically keeps the social proof fresh without you touching it. The Google profile collects the reviews; the site puts them in front of the visitor a referral or the map sent over.
